## 2. Literature Review

### 2.1 The Enteric Nervous System as the First Brain

The enteric nervous system (ENS) is an independent evolutionary innovation that predates the central nervous system. Furness et al. (2018) demonstrated that an ENS occurs in all animals investigated, including hydra, echinoderms, and hemichordates that do not have a CNS. The general form of the ENS, which consists of plexuses of neurons intrinsic to the gut wall and an innervation that controls muscle movements, is similar in species as varied and as far apart as hydra, sea cucumbers, annelid worms, octopus, and humans.

**Key Finding:** "We conclude that an ENS arose before and independently of the CNS. Thus the ENS can be regarded as the first brain" (Furness et al., 2018).

Neurochemical similarities across phyla imply a common origin of the ENS. The same neurotransmitters—acetylcholine, serotonin, dopamine, GABA—that operate in the enteric nervous system also operate in sperm flagella.

### 2.2 Shared Protein Machinery: Sperm and Intestine

#### 2.2.1 GLUT5: The Shared Fructose Transporter

Burant et al. (1992) identified that the glucose transporter isoform GLUT5 is expressed on the brush border membrane of human small intestinal enterocytes AND is the fructose transporter in human spermatozoa. RNA and protein blotting studies showed the presence of high levels of GLUT5 mRNA and protein in human testis and spermatozoa, and immunocytochemical studies localize GLUT5 to the plasma membrane of mature spermatids and spermatozoa.

**Key Finding:** The same protein that transports fructose in the intestine transports fructose in sperm, establishing a molecular continuum between gut and gamete.

#### 2.2.2 SPEF1/CLAMP: The Structural Bridge

Tapia et al. (2019) demonstrated that Sperm Flagellar 1 (SPEF1, also called CLAMP) was originally identified as a microtubule-associated protein regulating flagellar structure. However, research demonstrates it binds actin in human intestinal epithelial cells and contributes to filopodia and lamellipodia formation. CLAMP distribution changes during intestinal epithelial cell polarization, regulates the formation of filopodia, and appears to assist in the organization of actin bundles within lamellipodia of migrating intestinal epithelial cells.

**Key Finding:** SPEF1 is a single protein that serves multiple functions across sperm flagella, intestinal epithelium, and ciliated tissues—establishing the molecular architecture of the enteric reproductive continuum.

### 2.3 Post-Mitotic Origin of the Head Brain

Iwata, Casimir, and Vanderhaeghen (2020) demonstrated that neural stem cell daughter cell fate is NOT determined at the moment of division but can be altered for hours after mitosis. Research shows that depletion of ETC complex I and complex V in type I neuroblasts causes mitochondrial fragmentation. Opa1 depletion causes mitochondrial fragmentation and proliferation and differentiation defects in Drosophila type II neuroblasts and intestinal stem cells. Concurrent depletion of Drp1 with Opa1 restores mitochondrial fusion and alleviates the differentiation defects, underscoring the necessity of a fused mitochondrial morphology for proper stem cell differentiation.

**Key Finding:** The "head brain" develops after mitosis. The drive is encoded in the pre-cortical proteome. The enteric mind operates independently of cortical processing.

### 2.4 Egg Activation: The Calcium Wave

Kaneuchi, Wolfner, and Aigaki (2015) revealed that egg activation is initiated by a calcium wave that sweeps across the egg. "Wave(s) of increased cytosolic calcium sweep across the egg, causing egg activation." In *Drosophila*, egg activation is triggered by osmotic pressure and mechanical forces. Osmolarity-regulated swelling initiates egg activation, regulated by conserved osmoregulatory channels, aquaporins and DEGenerin/Epithelial Na⁺ channels.

**The Mechanistic Chain:**
1. Osmotic pressure causes swelling
2. Swelling triggers calcium entry
3. Calcium wave sweeps across the egg
4. Actin cytoskeleton reorganizes
5. Egg activates
