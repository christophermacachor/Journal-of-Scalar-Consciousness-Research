Abstract

Background: Near-death experiences (NDEs) exhibit a core phenomenological architecture, yet no systematic framework explains why this architecture varies between individuals. The Enterarchon Hypothesis (Macachor, 2026b) posits that the enteric nervous system (ENS) is the primary neural substrate of consciousness, with the central nervous system serving as a secondary processing layer connected via the vagus nerve. This article extends that framework to propose the Enterarchon Perinatal Imprint: the modality of birth imprints a distinct spatial template upon the ENS, which is reactivated during subsequent near-death events.

Methods: This theoretical framework synthesises: (1) the largest systematic qualitative analysis of NDEs (Hashemi et al., 2023; n = 465 NDErs across 54 studies, 1980–2022); (2) independent childbirth-related NDE case reports (Wistrand, 2012); (3) extended-coma veridical NDEs (Sepioni et al., 2024); (4) critical-care obstetric NDEs (Panditrao et al., 2010; Oldak et al., 2024); (5) verified physician NDE during childbirth (Woollacott & Peyton, 2021); (6) developmental neurobiology of the ENS (Furness, 2006, 2012; Le Douarin & Teillet, 1973); (7) microbiome-gut-brain axis research (Cryan et al., 2019; Dominguez-Bello et al., 2010); and (8) the Enterarchon Hypothesis corpus (Macachor, 2026a, 2026b).

Results: Six childbirth-related cases with determinable birth context were identified from the systematic corpus, supplemented by recent case reports from the obstetric literature. Stratification reveals a bifurcation: vaginal birth NDEs feature directional, compressive, tunnel-like phenomenology; cesarean section NDEs feature spatial, exposed, veridical surgical-field phenomenology. The Enterarchon framework reframes this bifurcation: the tunnel is not merely the birth canal, it is the compression of the enteric brain through the canal. The surgical field is not merely the opened abdomen, it is the exposure of the enteric brain when the abdominal cavity is opened. The vagus nerve, as the scalar channel between ENS and CNS, transmits these perinatal imprints to cortical memory.

Conclusion: The NDE is not a window into a universal afterlife but a reactivation of the first moment of enteric consciousness, filtered through the perinatal spatial template. The brain functions as a secondary receiver; the ENS is the primary scalar field transducer. This framework challenges both reductionist neurobiology and disembodied spiritualism, offering a predictive, empirically testable taxonomy for clinical NDE assessment grounded in enteric neuroanatomy and birth history.

1. Introduction: The Enterarchon Framework

The Enterarchon Hypothesis (Macachor, 2026b; Zenodo: 10.5281/zenodo.21868016) proposes a radical inversion of the standard neurocognitive hierarchy: the enteric nervous system (ENS), the "second brain" of the gastrointestinal tract, is not a peripheral outpost of the central nervous system (CNS) but its primary neural substrate. The CNS, including the cortex, functions as a secondary processing and integration layer. The vagus nerve is not a descending command pathway but an ascending scalar channel that transmits enteric field states to cortical awareness (Berthoud & Neuhuber, 2000; Craig, 2002).

This inversion is supported by developmental biology: the ENS is the first neural system to develop in the embryo, arising from neural crest cells that migrate to the gut wall before the brain has formed its first synapse (Furness, 2012; Gershon, 1998). The gut precedes the brain. The enteric brain is not secondary; it is primordial.

The present article extends the Enterarchon framework to the domain of near-death experiences (NDEs). If the ENS is the primary receiver of consciousness, then the first moment of independent ENS operation, birth, must imprint a spatial template that structures all subsequent non-local perception. The modality of that birth determines the template:

  • Vaginal birth compresses the enteric brain through the birth canal. The ENS experiences increasing pressure, constriction, and directional movement toward a single exit. This imprints a tunnel-gradient template.
  • Cesarean birth exposes the enteric brain when the abdominal cavity is opened. The ENS experiences sudden exposure, surgical light, and observation from above. This imprints an open-cavity template.

When death approaches and the CNS receiver fails, the ENS, as the primary scalar transducer, continues to perceive. But it perceives through its earliest spatial memory: the perinatal imprint. The tunnel is the compressed gut. The operating room is the exposed gut. Both are true. Neither is universal.

The birth-related NDE literature, while limited, provides crucial evidence for this framework. Bruce Greyson has documented approximately 60 to 70 cases of NDEs during labor and childbirth (Greyson, personal communication, cited in Lahood, 2016), yet systematic analysis of birth modality remains absent from the literature. This represents a critical gap in NDE research that the Enterarchon framework seeks to address.

The stages of birth, from the amniotic universe, through the engulfment of labor, the death-rebirth struggle, and final emergence into light, map remarkably onto the archetypal NDE sequence (Grof, 1975; Jansen, 1998).

The Enterarchon Perinatal Imprint extends this earlier work by providing a neurobiological mechanism: the ENS, as the primary receiver of consciousness, encodes the spatial gradient of birth at the moment of delivery, creating a template that structures subsequent NDE phenomenology. This explains why the "tunnel of light" is not a universal feature but a modality-specific imprint, a distinction missed by earlier birth-NDE analogies.

1.3 The Enteric Brain as Primary Receiver

1.3.1 Developmental Primacy

The human ENS contains approximately 500 million neurons, more than the spinal cord, organized into microcircuits capable of independent reflexive activity (Furness, 2006). These neurons arise from vagal neural crest cells that begin migrating to the foregut at embryonic week 4, before the cerebral cortex has begun to form (Le Douarin & Teillet, 1973). The gut has a functional nervous system before the brain has a functional cortex.

This developmental sequence is not incidental. It establishes the ENS as the original receiver of environmental information. The first sensations the developing organism experiences, pressure, chemical gradients, temperature, are enteric. The first spatial map is a gut map.

1.3.2 The Vagus as Scalar Channel

The vagus nerve (cranial nerve X) is conventionally described as a parasympathetic efferent pathway regulating heart rate, digestion, and respiration. But approximately 80% of vagal fibers are afferent, ascending from the viscera to the brainstem (Berthoud & Neuhuber, 2000). These afferents carry not merely physiological data but qualitative state information: gut feelings, visceral intuition, the somatic markers that precede conscious decision (Damasio, 1994).

In the Enterarchon framework, the vagus is the scalar channel through which the ENS transmits field states to the CNS. It is not a data line; it is a coherence conduit. The scalar field of consciousness is received first by the ENS, transmitted via the vagus to the brainstem, and only then integrated by cortical structures into the narrative self (Macachor, 2026b).

When the CNS fails, in cardiac arrest, coma, or pharmacological suppression, the scalar channel remains open. The ENS continues to receive. The vagus continues to transmit. But without the cortical integrator, the transmission is experienced as raw, unfiltered, and spatially structured by the earliest imprint.

1.3.3 The Microbiome as Scalar Dampener

The ENS does not operate in isolation. It is embedded in a microbial ecosystem of approximately 38 trillion organisms, collectively encoding a genomic library 150 times larger than the human genome (Sender et al., 2016). These microbes produce neuroactive compounds, GABA, serotonin, dopamine, short-chain fatty acids, that modulate ENS activity and, via the vagus, CNS function (Cryan et al., 2019).

In the Enterarchon framework, the microbiome functions as a scalar dampener, a noise filter that stabilizes the ENS field against environmental perturbation. This is not merely metaphorical. The microbial population creates a biochemical density gradient that shapes the scalar field's local equilibrium. When the microbiome is disrupted (dysbiosis), the dampener fails, and the ENS receives unfiltered scalar noise, manifesting as anxiety, dissociation, or, in extreme cases, uncontrolled non-local perception (Macachor, 2026b).

The perinatal imprint is encoded not merely by ENS neurons but by the first microbial colonization, the passage through the birth canal inoculates the infant with maternal vaginal microbiota, while cesarean birth inoculates with skin microbiota (Dominguez-Bello et al., 2010). The microbial signature of birth becomes part of the scalar dampener's baseline:

  • Vaginal birth = Lactobacillus-dominant, acidic, GABAergic. The acidic, GABAergic environment may contribute to peaceful NDE affect.
  • Cesarean birth = Staphylococcus-dominant, neutral, inflammatory. The neutral, inflammatory environment may contribute to distressing NDE affect.

2. Materials and Methods

2.1 Primary Corpus

The theoretical framework is built upon the systematic qualitative analysis by Hashemi et al. (2023), which reviewed 2,407 studies, included 54 for final analysis, and catalogued 465 NDErs across 27 case reports, 20 case series, and 7 qualitative studies (1980–2022). The review employed JBI Critical Appraisal Checklists and Graneheim-Lundman qualitative content analysis. Inclusion criteria required:

  • Explicit NDE documentation (Greyson Scale or equivalent)
  • Qualitative descriptions of NDE phenomenology
  • Available clinical context (etiology, medical status)
  • Publication in peer-reviewed journals

2.2 Childbirth Case Extraction

From the 54 studies, all cases with explicit childbirth, birth trauma, or cesarean section etiology were extracted and classified by:

  • Birth modality (vaginal vs. cesarean) where determinable
  • NDE architecture (tunnel/directional vs. exposed/spatial vs. mixed)
  • Enteric correlation (compression vs. exposure of abdominal cavity)
  • Veridical content (corroborated perceptions vs. transcendental/symbolic)
  • Affective valence (positive/beatific vs. negative/distressing)

2.3 Supplementary Sources

Independent case reports and the Enterarchon Hypothesis corpus (Macachor, 2026a, 2026b) were integrated to provide the scalar-field theoretical foundation. These include:

  • Wistrand (2012) childbirth NDEs (n = 5 women)
  • Pennachio (1988) cesarean NDE with three sequential experiences
  • Panditrao et al. (2010) cardiac arrest during cesarean
  • Sepioni et al. (2024) 20-day coma veridical NDE
  • Serdahely & Walker (1990) birth trauma NDE
  • Woollacott & Peyton (2021): Verified physician NDE during childbirth
  • Oldak et al. (2024): Six cardiac arrests during emergency cesarean
  • Janet Crawford (Project Profound, 2023): Uterine rupture, emergency C-section, cardiac arrest
  • Arnold (2014): Amniotic fluid embolism, 37 seconds flat-lined during delivery
  • Hermanstorfer (CNN, 2010): Cardiac arrest during labor, emergency C-section

2.4 The Birth Modality Gap

A significant limitation in the existing literature is the absence of systematic documentation of birth modality in NDE studies. Hashemi et al. (2023) did not record birth modality in their systematic review. Bruce Greyson has documented an estimated 60 to 70 cases of NDEs during labor and childbirth, but birth modality (vaginal vs. cesarean) was not systematically tracked (Greyson, personal communication, cited in Lahood, 2016).

This represents a critical gap that the Enterarchon framework seeks to address. The hypothesis predicts that birth modality will correlate with NDE architecture, and the available case evidence supports this prediction. Future research must collect birth modality data prospectively to empirically validate the framework.

3.2.3 The Criticisms of the Birth-NDE Analogy

The birth-NDE analogy has faced criticism. Blackmore (1993) concluded that the "tunnel of light" is not a repressed memory of vaginal birth, noting that individuals born by Cesarean section and free of the trauma of vaginal delivery still described a "tunnel of light" sensation. Similarly, Jansen (1998) noted that the birth trauma frequently appears as a core imprint described in four parts, but the specific tunnel-light sequence may represent a different mechanism.

The Enterarchon framework addresses this criticism by distinguishing between the general perinatal imprint (the first scalar gradient encoded by the ENS) and the specific modality of birth. The "tunnel of light" is not a universal feature; it is a modality-specific imprint. The fact that some Cesarean-born individuals report tunnel experiences does not falsify the framework, as the ENS may still encode a directional gradient from intrauterine life (the "amniotic universe" phase) or from the compression of the abdominal cavity during the procedure. The Enterarchon framework is more nuanced than the simple "birth canal = tunnel" analogy, it proposes that the ENS encodes all spatial gradients experienced during birth, and that different modalities produce different imprints.

3.5 Limitations and Future Directions

3.5.1 Limitations
  1. Birth modality was not systematically recorded in most NDE studies
  2. Only 6 childbirth-related cases with determinable context exist in the systematic corpus (Hashemi et al., 2023)
  3. Microbial analysis was not performed in any NDE study
  4. The scalar-field framework is theoretical and requires empirical testing via quantum biology methods
  5. Selection bias in NDE reporting may favor certain phenomenologies
  6. Retrospective recall of NDEs may be influenced by subsequent life events
  7. The Greyson corpus of 60–70 childbirth NDEs lacks birth modality documentation (Greyson, personal communication, cited in Lahood, 2016)
  8. Hybrid birth modalities (vaginal followed by C-section) are not systematically distinguished from pure birth modalities
3.5.2 Future Research

Prospective Studies:

  1. Birth modality collection: Prospectively collect birth modality, microbiome profile, and ENS function data from NDErs
  2. Greyson Scale correlation: Correlate Greyson Scale sub-scores with birth history and vagal tone
  3. FMT intervention: Investigate whether fecal microbiota transplantation alters NDE recall or phenomenology
  4. Water birth prediction: Test the prediction that individuals born via water birth (minimal compression, minimal exposure) will report NDEs with minimal spatial structure
  5. VNS during arrest: Examine whether vagal nerve stimulation during cardiac arrest produces NDE-like states
  6. Hybrid birth study: Analyze NDE architecture in individuals born via vaginal delivery followed by emergency C-section
  7. Obstetric NDE registry: Establish a registry of pregnancy-related NDEs that documents birth modality prospectively
