Abstract

The Obelisk—a recently characterized class of viroid-like RNA elements found in the human gut microbiome—represents an unresolved biological entity with unusual structural properties and uncertain functional significance (Zheludev et al., 2024, Cell). This manuscript applies the Macachor Structural Convergence Principle (MSCP) as an analytical framework to identify recurring structural and dynamical motifs across disparate biological systems, and proposes the Obelisk Hypothesis as a testable framework linking microbiome-associated RNA structures, enteric neural networks, and consciousness-related phenomenology.

Greyson and Pehlivanova (2025) identify unresolved explanatory questions within current neuroscientific models of near-death experiences (NDEs). This paper proposes the Enterarchon/Obelisk mechanism as a testable alternative hypothesis—grounded in empirically supported observations spanning embryology, neuroscience, reproductive biology, and consciousness studies, with the Obelisk integration explicitly marked as hypothesis-generating rather than established.

“The observations are established independently; their convergence motivates the present theoretical model. The proposed Obelisk–ENS mechanism is a falsifiable hypothesis rather than an established biological fact.”
— Christopher Macachor

II

The Obelisk Foundation

What Is Known

Obelisks are approximately 1-kb apparently circular RNAs with predicted rod-like secondary structures, found in human gut microbiomes (Zheludev et al., 2024, Cell).

  • Genomic structure: ~1,000 nucleotide circular RNA assemblies
  • Secondary structure: Predicted rod-like conformations
  • Protein coding: Some encode novel Oblin proteins
  • Self-cleaving motifs: A subset contains hammerhead ribozymes
  • Host association: Found in human microbiomes; biological role unresolved

What Remains Unresolved

“The biological function of Obelisks remains to be determined.”
— Nature Reviews Microbiology, 2024

This unresolved status is an opportunity. Ω-FRAG-007 frames Obelisks as a candidate mechanism for previously unrecognized enteric information processing.

Falsification Program

The Obelisk Hypothesis is presented as a testable, falsifiable scientific framework. Each prediction has a defined falsification criterion.

Prediction 1 — Obelisk Abundance and Neural State

Measure Obelisk abundance in human intestinal samples and simultaneously measure ENS-related physiological markers.

Prediction: Obelisk-positive and Obelisk-negative microbiomes should exhibit reproducible differences in enteric signaling.

Falsification: No reproducible differences after controlling for microbial-load effects.

Prediction 2 — Obelisk Perturbation

Use an appropriate experimental microbial system to increase or suppress a specific Obelisk-host association.

Prediction: Perturbation should produce measurable changes in enteric signaling independent of ordinary microbial-load effects.

Falsification: No Obelisk-dependent changes in enteric signaling.

Prediction 3 — RNA Structure

Determine experimentally whether the predicted Obelisk structures exist in vivo rather than relying solely on computational predictions.

Prediction: Experimental structure determination confirms rod-like conformations.

Falsification: In vivo structures differ from predictions.

Prediction 4 — Electromagnetic/Quantum Mechanism

If the antenna hypothesis is retained, specify the predicted frequency range, coupling mechanism, coherence lifetime, measurable physical observable, and expected signal-to-noise ratio.

Prediction: A reproducible physical signal is detectable.

Falsification: No reproducible signal above noise.

Prediction 5 — Birth-Modality Hypothesis

Pre-register: birth modality → NDE phenomenological dimensions, controlling for age, sex, cardiac-arrest circumstances, hypoxia duration, medications, cultural background, prior NDE expectations, memory characteristics, trauma, and time since event.

Prediction: Birth modality predicts NDE phenomenology after covariate adjustment.

Falsification: No association after covariate adjustment.

Explicit Falsification Statement

A failure to detect Obelisk-dependent enteric effects would count against the Obelisk–ENS mechanism. A failure of birth modality to predict NDE phenomenology after preregistered adjustment would count against the Perinatal Imprint Hypothesis. A failure to identify a reproducible physical coupling mechanism would count against the quantum-antenna interpretation.
